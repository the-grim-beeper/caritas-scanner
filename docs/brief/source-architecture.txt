Verification frame and main findings
Research cut: 8 October 2026. The project brief says 6 October 2026; I used the current system date, 8
October 2026, so the register includes material published through that date where it was visible.


The territorial premise is correct: Landkreis Miesbach has 17 municipalities. The county's own directory
confirms the set in the brief. 1 The March 2026 local elections materially changed the political layer that
the scanner must watch. The county notes that Gmund a. Tegernsee and Kreuth did not elect mayors in
March 2026 because their mayoral cycles are off-cycle. 2 Jens Zangenfeind was elected Landrat in the 22
March runoff with 78.0 percent and is now shown by the Landkreis as Landrat. 3 In Miesbach, Dr. Thomas
Acher won the mayoral runoff with 51.9 percent. 4


The primary-source check also verifies several other post-election office-holders without relying on pre-
election pages: Bayrischzell's current council page names Georg Kittenrainer as First Mayor; Irschenberg's
May 2026 municipal report identifies newly elected Florian Pichler; Tegernsee's council page from May 2026
identifies Johannes Hagn; and Waakirchen's new council page identifies Norbert Kerkel. 5 I did not obtain
a comparably current primary-source confirmation for every one of the other municipalities, so I have not
filled those names from memory or older pages.


The most important substantive finding for the scanner is that the county already contains more
coordination infrastructure than a superficial search suggests. The Fachstelle für Soziales und Senioren
publicly associates itself with Sozialplanung und Demografie, Sozialberichterstattung, the Sozialbeirat,
Netzwerk Ehrenamt, Netzwerk Quartiersmanagement, Netzwerk Integration and the Verbund Psychische
Gesundheit Region 17. It also lists a broad provider and civil-society environment. 6 The Pflegestützpunkt
provides another cross-organisational node, including regular advice from Bezirk Oberbayern, while the
GesundheitsregionPlus is currently described as “im Aufbau” rather than as an already mature network.
 7 This distinction matters: the scanner should watch not just the creation of named networks, but

whether they acquire mandates, recurring meetings, public work programmes and durable staffing.


There is also unusually strong fiscal reason to monitor several government levels together. Bezirk
Oberbayern's 2026 budget is €3.199 billion, with a 24.7-percent Bezirksumlage rate; the Bezirk says roughly
92.8 percent of its 2026 budget goes to social services. 8 Bavaria's 2026 kommunaler Finanzausgleich is
€12.83 billion, while the state explicitly acknowledges rapidly rising social expenditure at local and district
levels. 9 These sources should be read together with the Landkreis and municipal budgets because a
nominal increase in state transfers can coexist with a tightening of discretionary local social spending.


Two 2026 policy changes deserve immediate inclusion in any horizon scan. First, the federal SGB II regime
changed on 1 July 2026: Bürgergeld was renamed Grundsicherungsgeld and a large package of rules
concerning activation, cooperation, housing costs and Jobcenter practice came into force. Further

Welfare and civil society

The public provider layer is active enough to produce useful weak signals. Diakonie Rosenheim's site had a
5 October 2026 item and exposes a Miesbach-specific Kinder- und Jugendhilfe page; that page says its
ambulatory services are provided on behalf of the Jugendamt, making it useful both as a service source
and as network evidence. 42 BRK Kreisverband Miesbach's news page was current to 18 September 2026.
 43 Lebenshilfe Miesbach has both an active provider site and a particularly useful vacancy page, which is a

direct skilled-labour signal rather than a retrospective report. 44 AWO's Miesbach senior centre was
current to 25 September, the Hospizkreis calendar contains events through December, and
Nachbarschaftshilfe Warngau shows active 2026 organisational updates. 45


These should not be interpreted as neutral system reports. Their institutional interest is to explain and
legitimate their own work. They are strongest for closures/openings, vacancies, leadership changes, new
projects and explicit funding announcements and weaker for quantifying unmet demand, failed referrals
or financial distress before management chooses to disclose it.


A notable gap remains here: I did not verify a current public scanner endpoint for every VdK local
organisation, Paritätischer member, Tafel, Mehrgenerationenhaus, volunteer agency, Bürgerstiftung,
self-help group, Seniorenbeauftragte or Behindertenbeauftragte. The county Fachstelle page partly
compensates as a network directory, but it does not substitute for direct monitoring of those organisations.

Church, Bezirk and Bavaria

The Catholic scanner should have three separate levels rather than one “church” source: Dekanat
Miesbach, the archdiocesan structure/reform source, and the diocesan Caritas organisation. Dekanat
Miesbach currently covers eleven Pfarrverbände and has an explicit coordination function. 13 The Caritas
München-Freising Geschäftsberichte page currently carries the 2025/2026 report and is the most useful
public source for group-level finance, workforce and organisational restructuring. 46 The Protestant
counterpart for the Miesbach area is Dekanat Bad Tölz. 47


At Bezirk level, four sources form a coherent package: Sitzungstermine for decisions; Bezirkshaushalt for
money and Bezirksumlage; Sozialplanung for institutional redesign; and Sozialberichterstattung for
indicator trends. 48 The Sozialberichterstattung source is particularly promising because the Bezirk now
describes interactive dashboards filterable by region and year; this is much closer to the reusable evidence
infrastructure a scanner needs than a one-off PDF report. 49


At Land level, SeLA remains live, with funding for senior-oriented Quartierskonzepte, Nachbarschaftshilfen
and other local measures. 12 PflegesoNah also remains active in 2026. 11 The Bayerischer Landtag's
live sitting page exposes plenary and committee activity and already links its 2027 sitting plan. 50 The

Landesamt für Statistik should be retained for demographic baselines; its June 2026 municipal population
publication still identifies 17 municipalities in Landkreis Miesbach. 51


For ministries, the current Bavarian names in the verified sources are Bayerisches Staatsministerium für
Familie, Arbeit und Soziales (StMAS) and Bayerisches Staatsministerium für Gesundheit, Pflege und

At federal level, the verified current names relevant here are Bundesministerium für Arbeit und Soziales

Bundesministerium für Gesundheit landing page before the research cutoff and therefore mark that
element of the requested federal source set as still needing verification rather than silently treating the old
source list as current.


Peer cases

The strongest directly transferable case located is Gemeindeschwester plus in Rheinland-Pfalz. It began
as a model project, went through a “Verstetigungsphase,” and is now a Landesprogramm. The current
programme page says it is financed from state funds in the present 2025/26 funding period and describes
both individual navigation and the role of the Fachkräfte in stimulating local supportive infrastructure. 54
This is unusually relevant to Miesbach because it makes institutional anchoring itself observable: model →
transition/continuation → regular state programme.


Two additional peer-document sources belong in the register even though they should be treated as
periodic research rather than weekly news: the Austrian Community Nursing continuation material, which
explicitly addresses what happened after the 2022–24 pilot phase, and the ISA work on kommunale
Präventionsketten, which examines resilience and institutionalisation across municipalities. 55 The
Austrian example is useful precisely because continuation varies by Land and Träger rather than following
one national template.


I did not verify adequate current primary documentation in this pass for all of the other requested
analogues—Bavarian Dorfkümmerer, rural Bavarian Quartiersmanagement cases with documented post-
grant outcomes, English social-prescribing link workers, and Dutch neighbourhood teams. They should stay
on the research backlog rather than being represented by unsourced textbook descriptions.

Gaps and uncertainty
The largest blind spot is not statistical. It is relational. Public sources can show that a Sozialbeirat or
Netzwerk Quartiersmanagement exists, but generally do not show who actually comes, who brokers
disagreements, who shares information by phone before a meeting, whether one organisation routinely
blocks decisions, or whether a network survives when one key individual leaves. The county's public
network map is therefore evidence of institutional form, not evidence of network function. 32

The same applies to several Layer-6/7 umbrella organisations requested in the brief—Bayerischer
Landkreistag, Bayerischer Gemeindetag, Freie Wohlfahrtspflege Bayern, Landes-Caritasverband,

The peer-case layer is intentionally narrow. Gemeindeschwester plus is the strongest verified
continuation case because the programme itself publicly documents the transition from model to
Verstetigung to Landesprogramm. 54 Austrian Community Nursing and Präventionsketten add useful
comparative material, but I am less certain about direct institutional transferability to rural Upper Bavaria. I
am still less certain about the requested English link-worker and Dutch neighbourhood-team comparisons
because I did not obtain sufficiently current primary-source evidence on post-project financing and
institutional survival in this pass.


Finally, the project-specific public record remains the largest factual uncertainty. I found enough evidence to
map the landscape around “Interkommunale Sozialraumlotsen,” but not enough public material to verify
the project's own network, contracts, financing, steering arrangements or intended
Verstetigungsmodell. That is precisely where the horizon scanner should not substitute inference for
interviews. The public record can tell Caritas when the environment is changing; Caritas and its partners will
have to provide the private institutional facts needed to judge whether the coordination model is actually
becoming durable.
